**Homepage visual review — September 7, 2026**

Reviewed the current local [homepage](http://localhost:5173/) at 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844, including the full page by scrolling, both product-showcase slides, desktop pointer decoration, and the mobile navigation drawer. Copy was excluded from the assessment; typography, text density, and text treatments were considered only as visual elements.

The homepage has a useful foundation: a recognizable header, clear primary buttons, readable sans-serif typography, a restrained base palette, and a conventional footer. My assessment is that it feels less established because its imagery and presentation vary substantially between sections. The scenic hero, animal animations, moving cards, glossy interface illustrations, and oversized icons compete to define the brand. A more cohesive presentation built around the actual product would make it feel more trustworthy and contemporary.

The recommendations below are ordered by priority. They are design judgments from this inspection, rather than findings from a user study.

1. **High — Make the product the main hero visual.** The savanna fills most of the first screen without visually identifying the software or its output. Replace the scenic background with a crisp view of the actual recording or response interface, supported by a white or subtle neutral surface. Use either a balanced split layout or a compact centered introduction followed by the product view. This would establish the product category immediately and give visitors something concrete to inspect. [Desktop evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/desktop-hero.png)

2. **High — Replace the monkey imagery in the managed-service comparison.** The large animal scenes create a comic tone in a section presenting a professional service. Use current workflow captures or a restrained process illustration consistent with the product interface. Keep the comparison visually balanced, with matching image proportions and aligned card content; the primary option can retain its existing accent treatment. This would give the section a more composed, professional appearance. [Desktop evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/desktop-managed-comparison.png) · [Mobile evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/mobile-managed-comparison.png)

3. **High — Give the trust strip a stable, recognizable visual format.** It currently appears as large moving text cards, with cards cut off at both viewport edges. On mobile, even a single identity can be only partly visible. Present the existing project information in a static, aligned grid with app icons or thumbnails. If approved customer logos are available, a compact row of consistently sized marks is another option. Recognizable identities and a stable layout would make this section easier to assess visually. [Desktop evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/desktop-trust-strip.png) · [Mobile evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/mobile-trust-strip.png)

4. **High — Use one authentic visual treatment for product previews.** The two showcase slides have different treatments: one resembles an annotated app screen, while the other uses floating panels, saturated blue, a gradient number, and a glow. Rebuild the visual presentation around current product captures with consistent scale, framing, color, and annotation style. Keep any annotations outside the interface they explain. This would make the previews feel like evidence of a coherent product. [Showcase evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/desktop-product-showcase.png) · [Second slide](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/desktop-showcase-second.png)

5. **Medium — Reduce competing motion and use conventional showcase controls.** The page combines pointer-triggered floating snippets, a moving trust strip, an automatically rotating showcase with a perimeter progress effect, and animated animal images. Prefer stable content and deliberate transitions initiated by the visitor. For the two showcase states, use recognizable tabs with a clear selected state, or display both states in sequence. The two small dots currently provide weak visual orientation. A calmer page would feel more predictable and controlled.

6. **Medium — Design a separate mobile crop for the product preview.** At 390 × 844, the desktop composition is scaled into a small image inside several layers of framing. Interface details become too small to inspect, even though the surrounding section occupies substantial vertical space. Show one meaningful product detail at a time, crop out incidental chrome, and reduce the surrounding inset. This would let mobile visitors recognize the actual interface rather than seeing only a miniature diagram. [Mobile evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/mobile-product-showcase.png)

7. **Medium — Shorten the hero and bring supporting evidence closer.** The first supporting section begins around 933 CSS pixels down on desktop and 877 on mobile, beyond the initial viewport in both reviewed sizes. Reduce purely decorative space above and below the hero content and let its height follow the content more closely. Keep the headline and action comfortably spaced while bringing the beginning of product or trust evidence nearer the first screen. [Desktop evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/desktop-hero.png) · [Mobile evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/mobile-hero.png)

8. **Medium — Make the hero form a more deliberate visual group.** On desktop, the narrow input-and-button stack floats within a much larger image and headline composition. Align it with the chosen hero content column, or give the centered content a clearly bounded width. Show the existing field label persistently above the input and preserve the matching input/button dimensions. This would make the starting action look more familiar and intentional without changing its behavior. [Evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/desktop-hero.png)

9. **Medium — Simplify container layers and tighten section rhythm.** The showcase has an outer rounded surface, an inset image frame, internal rounded panels, shadows, and an animated border. Large gaps separate it from adjacent sections. Use one principal surface per section, retain additional borders only where they clarify grouping, and apply a consistent spacing rhythm between section headings, visuals, and supporting text. The result should feel spacious but more continuous and less assembled from separate templates. [Evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/desktop-showcase-second.png)

10. **Medium — Normalize icon style and make mobile features more compact.** The feature row combines large outline symbols with solid platform logos. Their size gives them more visual weight than their supporting role warrants. Use consistently sized icons with comparable stroke weight; keep platform marks as secondary details where needed. On mobile, consider compact rows with an icon beside the existing text. The current three-feature section occupies roughly 1,030 CSS pixels, creating a long interruption in the page's flow. [Desktop evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/desktop-feature-grid.png) · [Mobile evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/mobile-feature-grid.png)

11. **Lower — Restrain promotional typography treatments.** The hero's marker-style highlight and the showcase heading's crossed-out/accented treatment add another visual language to otherwise clean typography. Reduce the prominence of these embellishments and let the established type scale and weight carry the hierarchy. This recommendation concerns styling only; wording is outside this review. A more restrained treatment would fit the professional product direction. [Hero evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/desktop-hero.png) · [Heading evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/mobile-product-showcase.png)

12. **Lower — Give the closing action a more intentional composition.** Most major sections are centered; the final action switches to a small left-aligned group within a broad open area. Compose it as a compact centered closing block, or a balanced desktop row that follows the page grid. Keep its mobile grouping tight and align its spacing with the footer. Preserve the footer's familiar brand, navigation, and support structure while refining its column spacing. [Desktop evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/desktop-footer.png) · [Mobile evidence](./homepage-visual-review-2026-09-07/mobile-footer.png)

Preserve the current logo, clean header, blue primary-action hierarchy, legible type family, white canvas, and recognizable mobile drawer. The desired modern character should come from crisp product imagery, coherent proportions, consistent icons, and precise spacing. Use the existing Test4Test components and semantic tokens for any later design work.

The general rationale is supported by Nielsen Norman Group's guidance connecting appropriate imagery, organization, and design quality with perceived [trustworthiness](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/), and its guidance on [scale, hierarchy, balance, contrast, and grouping](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/). The page-specific observations and priorities above are my assessment.

Review only. No implementation was performed. Application files, product behavior, tokens, and visual baselines were not changed by this review. Evidence consists of viewport screenshots of the current local working version; this is not a production, accessibility, or release-validation sign-off.
